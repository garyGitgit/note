Three findings deserve emphasis:\textbf{(1) Semantic typing recovers exactly what regex typing cannot.} The regex classifier collapses 39.6\% of episodes (490/1,237) into an uninformative ``other'' error type. On the 338 test queries drawn from this subset, the LLM-compiled layer reaches \textbf{30.8\% Top-1} --- statistically indistinguishable from its overall 33.0\% --- demonstrating that semantic error typing restores discriminative power precisely where signature-based matching is blind (e.g., ``unconnected signal path'', ``retention register not held'', ``frequency below target margin'' --- all typed ``other'' by the regex classifier).\textbf{(2) Root-cause class is the most stable semantic dimension.} Signal analysis shows the block+semantic-type signal fired 5,686 times and block+root-cause-class 3,396 times in top-10 lists; ranking candidates by root-cause-class agreement within a semantic group was the single largest contributor to the Top-1 gain. This validates the multi-signal design: the LLM's per-episode root-cause classification (8 classes) is more consistent across episodes than its 17-class semantic error taxonomy, and the retrieval score exploits both.\textbf{(3) Compile-time LLM cost, query-time determinism.} The LLM performs $\sim$1,237 one-time episode distillations (a batched, offline process taking hours, not days), after which query-time behavior is fully deterministic at 0.32\,ms mean latency --- 12.8$\times$ faster than the signature layer's 4.1\,ms (which pays for hash reconstruction and multi-signal arithmetic on regex tokenization). In a live system, one additional LLM call per incoming failure produces its semantic signature; the per-failure amortized cost is one inference, with zero LLM usage on the retrieval path. Compilation quality is storage-time work: an incorrect semantic typing is a compile-time artifact that can be corrected once and benefits every future query, unlike query-time LLM reclassification errors which are re-made on every query.
