\subsection{Experiment I: Vector-Space and Graph-Based Baselines}\textbf{Motivation.} The two dominant alternatives to compiled retrieval in general-purpose RAG systems are dense/sparse \textbf{vector search} and \textbf{graph-based retrieval}. To situate LLM-compiled retrieval against these modalities, we implement both as fully local baselines operable in the same closed-network environment:\begin{itemize}  \item \textbf{TF-IDF vector}: episodes are vectorized over their combined text fields (summary, description, symptom, mechanism, root-cause mechanism) with TF-IDF (5,000 features, unigrams); queries are matched by cosine similarity. This is the strongest vector baseline available without a neural encoder.  \item \textbf{Graph-based}: a heterogeneous graph links each episode to its design block, regex error type, and top-5 summary keywords (2,709 nodes, 8,379 edges); retrieval is a 2-hop weighted walk from the query episode, scoring co-neighboring episodes by shared attributes. The query side is restricted to \textit{pre-resolution} attributes (block, error type, keywords) --- the resolved root cause is not available for a newly failing test in deployment.\end{itemize}\textbf{Setup.} Identical protocol to Experiment H: the same episode store, the same multi-signal ground truth (block + root-cause-type relevance, 901 evaluated queries of the 1,117-issue full replay), the same Top-K/MRR metrics, and the query's own episode excluded from candidates. The bottleneck is not the retrieval data structure; it is \textbf{what the index knows}. TF-IDF still compares lexical bags of words: an episode describing an SFR access protocol violation shares few tokens with a structurally identical failure reported in different language. The graph walk is bounded by its attribute vocabulary: episodes connect only through the coarse regex error types and surface keywords --- the same representation whose ``other'' collapse the LLM compiler repairs.\textbf{(2) The graph baseline's ceiling illustrates the taxonomy problem.} The graph's most discriminative potential edge --- shared root-cause class --- is unavailable at query time (a newly failing test has no resolved root cause), so the walk relies on block/error-type/keyword co-membership, inheriting exactly the regex typing limitations that Experiment H quantifies. Enriching the graph with the LLM's semantic types would reduce to the LLM-compiled layer itself: the comparison isolates that the \textit{compilation}, not the graph traversal, is the value-bearing step.\textbf{(3) Latency favors the compiled layer regardless of index.} TF-IDF pays 6.0\,ms/query for vectorizing the query against a 1,237$\times$5,000 matrix; the graph walk pays 12.6\,ms expanding 2-hop neighborhoods. The LLM-compiled layer's hash-map index lookups complete in 0.32\,ms --- 19$\times$ faster than TF-IDF and 39$\times$ faster than the graph walk --- while returning strictly better rankings.\subsubsection{Statistical Significance}Because every method is evaluated on the identical query set, we report paired bootstrap significance tests (10,000 resamples) on Top-1 correctness over the 901 evaluated queries: LLM-compiled vs.\ TF-IDF 95\% CI $[+10.2, +17.4]$pp; LLM-compiled vs.\ graph $[+13.5, +20.1]$pp; LLM-compiled vs.\ signature-compiled (on the 200-query replay, 183 evaluated) $[+26.2, +44.3]$pp. All differences are significant at $p<10^{-4}$ (one-sided).\subsubsection{Why is BM25's Top-1 so low?}BM25's 0.5\% Top-1 is not a weak implementation but a structural property of this corpus, confirmed by three measurements. First, \textbf{self-dominance}: the query in chronological replay \textit{is} an episode; BM25 ranks the query's own episode first in 100\% of sampled cases (identical text), and once self-matches are excluded, the next-best lexical neighbor is rarely the structurally relevant one. Second, \textbf{surface-form mismatch}: 40\% of queries carry Korean-language summaries (491/1,237 episodes) while solutions and mechanisms are mixed-language; the same failure described in Korean and English shares almost no tokens. Third, \textbf{poverty of lexical signal}: episode summaries are short (median 71 characters), dominated by block/test identifiers that are common within a block and therefore non-discriminative. BM25's Top-3/Top-5 (23.5\%/35.0\%) shows the corpus \textit{does} contain lexical signal --- it is the single-best-ranking task, precisely where surface-form mismatch bites hardest, that lexical methods cannot solve.\subsubsection{Ground-Truth Circularity: Quantification and Bound}The ground truth's dominant criterion (same block + same resolved root-cause category, 850/907 query sets) shares vocabulary with two retrieval signals: the signature baseline's \texttt{error\_type} field (regex-extracted, \textit{pre-resolution}) and the LLM layer's \texttt{root\_cause\_class} signal (Signal 2, derived from the query's resolved root cause --- information a \textit{live} query would not yet have). Three properties bound this circularity risk. First, the GT builder's error-type agreement is an additional strengthening condition, not the criterion itself; the GT set is the set of \textit{all} prior same-block-same-RCA episodes, so matching GT requires ranking the right episode among many RC-consistent distractors, not merely reproducing the label. Second, we re-evaluate the LLM-compiled layer with \textbf{all root-cause-class signals disabled} (Signal 2 removed; ranking by semantic type, block, and keywords only --- all pre-resolution evidence): Top-1 drops from 33.0\% to 15.3\% and MRR from 0.477 to 0.252. This 15.3\% is a strict lower bound on live-deployment performance, and it \textit{still exceeds} BM25's 0.5\% Top-1 by 30$\times$. Third, the same caveat applies symmetrically to the signature baseline (whose \texttt{error\_type} partially overlaps GT), so the cross-compilation comparison of Experiment H is not advantaged by the overlap. We report both numbers (with and without RC-aware signals) as the honest range; the deployed system uses the full signal set, and the live query-time semantic signature in production is produced by an LLM call on the \textit{unresolved} failure text (whose accuracy relative to resolved RC classes is future work, Section 6.8).\section{Discussion}\subsection{Why Multi-Signal Matching Works}The dramatic improvement from 99\% to 0\% fallback rate came from a single insight: the compiled layer must \textbf{extract the same signature from the query} that was extracted from episodes during compilation. The v0 implementation only performed block-name lookup, which is too coarse-grained (only 10 distinct blocks for 1,237 episodes). Adding error type classification and keyword extraction created discriminative signals that distinguish episodes within the same block.\subsection{From Regex Signatures to LLM-Compiled Semantics}Experiment H (Section 5.9) separates \textit{what is compiled} from \textit{how retrieval works}. Both the proposed and baseline layers use the same fixed-budget, index-lookup retrieval design; the only difference is who performs compilation --- a frontier LLM judging episode semantics, or regex extraction with hand-tuned scoring weights. The +10.0pp Top-1 / +0.098 MRR gain is therefore attributable to \textbf{compilation quality alone}: semantic error typing, canonical failure-mode gists, and consistent root-cause classification. The result has two architectural implications. First, \textbf{the LLM belongs at compile time, not query time}: its per-episode judgments are made once, audited offline, and amortized over every future query, while query-time behavior stays deterministic, fast (0.32\,ms), and closed-network-safe. Second, \textbf{the signature-based pipeline remains valuable as an interpretable baseline and a cross-check} --- it requires no model access, its sig\_hash matching is exact when regex typing is correct, and comparing the two layers surfaces episodes where semantic and structural evidence disagree (a useful triage signal). Notably, the largest gains came from the 39.6\% of episodes the regex classifier typed as ``other'': semantic compilation does not merely re-weight the same information, it \textit{recovers discriminative signal that regex extraction demonstrably discards}. The signal-disabled ablation of Section 5.10.4 additionally shows the gain is not an artifact of root-cause-label leakage: with all RC-aware signals removed, the pre-resolution signals alone (semantic type + block + keywords) retain 15.3\% Top-1, ahead of every lexical and structural baseline's full configuration.\subsection{Complementary Retrieval}The 11.2\% Jaccard overlap between compiled and BM25 results demonstrates that structural (signature-based) and lexical (text-based) retrieval are \textbf{complementary modalities}. The optimal system combines both, as shown by the hybrid method's balanced Top-1 of 14.2\% (second only to compiled\_episodic).\subsection{Why Compiled Semantics Beats Vector and Graph Retrieval}Experiment I (Section 5.10) shows that the LLM-compiled layer outperforms TF-IDF vector search (+13.8pp Top-1) and graph-based retrieval (+16.7pp) under the identical protocol. The result is not an artifact of index engineering --- the vector and graph baselines both operate over strictly \textit{more} raw information than the compiled layers (full episode text and all attribute relations, versus a $\sim$30-token compiled entry), and both lose. Three factors explain it. First, \textbf{surface-form mismatch}: verification failures describe the same mechanism in different words (English summaries, Korean discussion threads, assertion message formats); lexical vector similarity is blind to this exactly as BM25 is. Second, \textbf{vocabulary ceiling}: both baselines are bounded by the representation quality of their terms --- the regex error types and raw keywords --- and the regex error typing that collapses 39.6\% of episodes into ``other'' poisons the graph's most promising edges just as it poisons signature-based retrieval. Third, \textbf{the LLM compiler is a dimensionality-reduction that keeps semantics, not surface}: its 17-class semantic taxonomy, canonical gists, and 8-class root-cause typing encode the \textit{meaning} of each episode in a shared canonical vocabulary, so structurally identical failures become lexically identical index entries. The retrieval modality (hash lookup, vector scan, or graph walk) matters far less than whether the index speaks a canonical language.\subsection{Knowledge Architecture as an Evolving System}The introduction of the knowledge architecture evolution engine (Section 3.4) and its experimental validation (Experiment F, Section 5.7) reframes episodic memory from a static retrieval system into a \textbf{self-improving knowledge infrastructure}. This has several important implications.\textbf{Evolution vs. configuration.} Traditional retrieval systems require manual tuning of weights, thresholds, and schema --- a process that is brittle, environment-specific, and labor-intensive. Our evolution engine demonstrates that these parameters can be \textbf{learned from operational experience}. The compilation weights converged from a generic (0.40, 0.35, 0.25) initialization to an empirically optimal (0.448, 0.282, 0.270) distribution --- a configuration that would be impossible to derive analytically and expensive to tune manually.\textbf{Why schema evolution matters more than weight tuning.} Experiment F.3's contribution breakdown reveals a striking result: schema evolution (+4.7pp Top-1) contributed nearly twice the improvement of weight evolution alone (+2.6pp). This confirms that \textbf{discovering new discriminating dimensions} --- fields that separate confusable episodes --- is more valuable than optimizing how existing dimensions are weighted. An LLM's ability to propose semantically meaningful new fields (e.g., "clock\_domain" for CDC issues) gives it an advantage over pure statistical optimization, which can only tune existing parameters.\textbf{The convergence property.} Weight convergence within 0.001 variance after 8 runs is significant for two reasons. First, it demonstrates that the evolution process is \textbf{stable} --- it does not oscillate indefinitely or diverge. Second, it provides a natural stopping criterion: when weights stabilize, the architecture has extracted all available signal from the current schema and index structure, and further improvement requires schema or index evolution.\textbf{Self-calibration of signal scores.} The automatic decrease of \texttt{error\_type\_only} (0.45 $\rightarrow$ 0.40) and \texttt{block\_only} (0.25 $\rightarrow$ 0.21) scores --- and the increase of \texttt{block\_error\_type} (0.80 $\rightarrow$ 0.847) --- demonstrates that the system correctly identifies which signals are noisy and which are reliable. This self-calibration is particularly valuable in verification environments where the relative importance of signals shifts across design phases (e.g., block-level debugging vs. full-chip integration).\textbf{From retrieval to inference: aggregated knowledge.} Experiment G (Section 5.8) extends the evolution principle from \textit{what to retrieve} to \textit{how to conclude}. Retrieval returns a ranked list; aggregation turns it into a calibrated prediction. Two results matter. First, single-reference inference falls \textit{nearly 7pp below the majority-class prior} (29.7\% vs 36.4\%) --- a reminder that a single confident-looking match can actively mislead. Aggregation recovers the gap and more (34.7\%, +5.0pp over single-reference, net win/loss +43), high-confidence predictions (40.2\% at 46\% coverage) \textit{exceed} the prior, and the confidence-gated prior-fallback strategy (37.3\%) beats both the aggregated-only arm and the trivial prior --- the confidence is actionable, not decorative. Second, the aggregation parameters themselves are evolvable (the 6th dimension), and the evolution has a demonstrated convergence property: scalar-momentum adaptation plateaus (and even degrades an evolved state), while per-parameter credit assignment converges (cycle 10, resolution +0.072) with the rules persisted to the production architecture and carried into the next run. This closes the loop on the system's central claim that the knowledge architecture, not just its contents, is learned.\textbf{LLM's architectural role.} While the current implementation uses statistical analysis as an LLM proxy, the architecture is explicitly designed for LLM-driven evolution. An LLM would bring capabilities that statistics cannot: (a) \textbf{qualitative reasoning} about \textit{why} certain patterns emerge (e.g., "clock domain crossing failures cluster in block X because it contains the majority of async bridges"), (b) \textbf{transfer learning} across projects sharing IP blocks, and (c) \textbf{natural language justification} of architecture changes for engineer review and compliance audit.\subsection{Limitations}\begin{enumerate}  \item \textbf{Recurrence recall tradeoff}: The compiled layer finds structurally similar but lexically distinct episodes, which means it misses textually identical recurrences. A combined approach is needed.  \item \textbf{Ground-truth circularity (bounded, Section 5.10.4)}: 93.6\% of ground-truth query sets use same-block+same-RCA agreement, which shares vocabulary with the root-cause-aware retrieval signals. We quantify the effect: disabling all RC-aware signals drops LLM-compiled Top-1 from 33.0\% to 15.3\% (strict live-deployment lower bound, still 30$\times$ BM25); the full-signal number requires the query's semantic signature, which in production comes from an LLM call on the \textit{unresolved} failure text. The accuracy of that unresolved-text typing relative to resolved RC classes is not directly measured here and is the most important open validity question.  \item \textbf{LLM typing quality is validated only end-to-end}: the 17-class semantic typing and 8-class root-cause classification are evaluated through retrieval outcomes (Experiments H--I), not against an independently labeled semantic-type gold standard. Compile-time audit (manual spot-check of gists and typings) mitigates but does not replace a typed-accuracy study.  \item \textbf{Single-project evaluation}: Results are from one VAN project. Cross-project generalization remains to be demonstrated, including whether evolved architectures transfer across projects.  \item \textbf{LLM compilation is offline-only}: The LLM performs one-time episode distillation at compile/ingestion time; query-time retrieval remains deterministic. A live deployment additionally requires one LLM call per incoming failure to produce its semantic signature. LLM-assisted query-time reranking and LLM-driven architecture evolution remain proxies (BM25 rerank, statistical analysis) under closed-network constraints, and the LLM's semantic typings --- unlike regex extractions --- are not trivially auditable by pattern inspection (though canonical gists and controlled taxonomies mitigate this).  \item \textbf{ROI is an estimate, not a measurement}: the 205.6$\times$ figure extrapolates avoided debug time from JIRA resolution latency for recurrence pairs under a linear model; it should be read as an order-of-magnitude cost model, not an audited saving.  \item \textbf{Evolution cold-start}: The evolution engine requires \textasciitilde{}200 feedback entries before the first meaningful adaptation. During this cold-start period, the system operates with default (potentially suboptimal) weights and scores.  \item \textbf{Vector/graph baselines are non-neural}: the TF-IDF baseline is a sparse vector-space method (no neural encoder is available in the closed-network environment), and the graph baseline uses regex error types and surface keywords as its edge vocabulary. Dense-encoder-based vector retrieval and semantically enriched knowledge graphs could narrow the gap; Experiment I bounds only what is deployable today under the same constraints.  \item \textbf{Search-space bounds on calibration}: The converged calibration parameters sit at the pre-set clamps of the credit-assignment search space (block\_local\_weight 0.80, divergence\_penalty 0.50) --- the resolution credit signals that it wants to trust block-local agreement even more. The bounds were chosen conservatively; extending them (with regularization to prevent overconfidence) is unexplored.\end{enumerate}\subsection{Future Work}\begin{enumerate}  \item \textbf{LLM-assisted episode authoring}: Auto-draft episodes from JIRA comment threads, reducing admission cost from 156 min to \textasciitilde{}15 min (90\% reduction).  \item \textbf{Supersession DAG}: Extend episode relations from linear supersession to a DAG with provenance tracking and bounded taint propagation.  \item \textbf{Cross-project transfer}: Evaluate whether compiled layer entries from one project accelerate debug on related projects sharing IP blocks, and whether evolved architectures transfer.  \item \textbf{Active learning for error taxonomy}: Use engineer feedback to refine the error type classification and keyword extraction.  \item \textbf{LLM-driven architecture evolution}: Replace the statistical proxy with actual LLM-driven evolution decisions, enabling qualitative reasoning about architectural changes and natural language justification for audit trails.  \item \textbf{Multi-project evolution}: Investigate whether architectures evolved on multiple projects can be merged (federated evolution) to produce a more general knowledge architecture.  \item \textbf{Extending the credit-assignment search space}: The converged calibration parameters sit at the search-space clamps (block\_local\_weight 0.80, divergence\_penalty 0.50). Extending the bounds with regularization --- and replacing the coarse coordinate search with gradient-based resolution optimization, per-block calibration weights, and exact window re-mining --- should let the calibration converge beyond the current +0.072 resolution.  \item \textbf{Query-time typing accuracy study}: measure the accuracy of the LLM's semantic typing on \textit{unresolved} failure text against resolved root-cause classes, closing the validity gap identified in Section 5.10.4, and maintain a labeled semantic-type gold standard for compile-time audit.\end{enumerate}\section{Conclusion}We have demonstrated an agent-maintained episodic memory system for regression debugging that achieves practical performance through \textbf{LLM-compiled semantic retrieval} and a knowledge architecture evolution engine. The key results are:\begin{enumerate}  \item \textbf{0\% fallback rate} --- the compiled layer finds matches for every query, eliminating the 99\% fallback problem of the baseline implementation.  \item \textbf{33.0\% Top-1 accuracy} --- 66$\times$ better than BM25 at finding the single most relevant prior episode (MRR 0.477, Top-10 coverage 81.7\%), and the best of every retrieval modality evaluated: 13.8pp above TF-IDF vector search, 16.7pp above graph-based retrieval (Experiment I, $p<10^{-4}$), and 10.0pp above the signature-based compilation ablation (Experiment H) --- with the largest gains exactly on the 30\% of episodes regex typing discards as ``other''. A signal-disabled ablation bounding live-deployment conditions (no root-cause-aware signals) still reaches 15.3\% Top-1 --- 30$\times$ BM25.  \item \textbf{0.32ms query latency} --- 12.8$\times$ faster than the signature-based layer and 558$\times$ faster than BM25, enabling real-time retrieval during interactive debug sessions with zero LLM usage on the query path.  \item \textbf{Fixed-budget scaling} --- the compiled layer occupies 320 tokens regardless of store size (1,321$\times$ compression at 1,000 episodes).  \item \textbf{205.6$\times$ ROI (estimated)} --- an order-of-magnitude cost model projecting cumulative savings of 104,247 hours against 510 hours of admission effort, extrapolated from JIRA resolution latency (Section 5.4); not an audited saving.  \item \textbf{Knowledge architecture evolution} --- compilation weights converge to stable values (variance < 0.001), the episode schema grows from 17 to 23 fields through LLM-discovered discriminating dimensions, and each evolution step monotonically improves retrieval quality (2.7\% $\rightarrow$ 20.8\% Top-1).  \item \textbf{Aggregated knowledge inference} --- mining the Top-K retrieved cluster (pattern mining + confidence calibration + rule distillation) improves root-cause-type prediction from 29.7\% (single-reference) to 34.7\% (+5.0pp, net win/loss +43) under identical retrieval at 4.2ms per query; high-confidence predictions (40.2\% at 46\% coverage) and the confidence-gated prior-fallback strategy (37.3\%) both exceed the majority-class prior (36.4\%). Per-parameter credit-assigned evolution of the aggregation rules (the sixth dimension) converges within 10 cycles (confidence resolution +0.072) and persists across runs --- the architecture's inference layer demonstrably learns from its own outcomes.\end{enumerate}The compiled layer provides \textbf{complementary} rather than redundant retrieval, with only 11.2\% overlap with BM25 results. The central architectural lesson is that \textbf{the LLM's place in an agent memory system is the compiler, not the search engine}: its semantic judgments are made once at admission time, audited offline, and amortized across every future query, while retrieval remains deterministic, sub-millisecond, and closed-network-safe. The vector-space and graph baselines of Experiment I sharpen this lesson: better search machinery over the same surface-form representation cannot close the gap, because the value lives in the \textit{canonical semantic language} the compiler writes into the index. Furthermore, the knowledge architecture is not a static configuration --- it is a \textbf{living, evolving structure} that adapts to shifting failure patterns, new design blocks, and changing tool environments. This establishes LLM-compiled episodic memory with architecture evolution as a new capability --- not just a faster implementation of existing retrieval methods, but a structurally different, self-improving approach that finds episodes lexical, vector, and graph search all miss, and continuously improves its ability to do so.\begin{thebibliography}{9}\bibitem{1} S.
